    \chapter{大規模脳ネットワークの力学：トリプルネットワーク・モデル}

    20世紀末からの安静時機能的磁気共鳴画像法（rs-fMRI）の進展は、脳科学に「機能局在論」から「大規模機能的結合ネットワーク（Large-Scale Brain Networks）」へのパラダイムシフトをもたらした。脳は孤立した機能野の集合ではなく、遠隔に分散する領域群が同期振動する動的システムとして作動する。
    その中核を担うのが、以下の3大ネットワークである。

    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{DMN（デフォルト・モード・ネットワーク / Default Mode Network）}：\\
            安静時・内省時に同期し、タスク遂行時に活動が減弱（タスク負相関）する領域群（mPFC, PCC, IPLなど）。自己参照的思考、エピソード記憶の想起、未来シミュレーション、および自己物語（ナラティブ）の構築を司る。
        \item \textbf{CEN（中央実行系ネットワーク / Central Executive Network）}：\\
            背外側前頭前皮質（dlPFC）および後頭頂皮質（PPC）を中心とし、ワーキングメモリ、論理的推論、問題解決など、外部タスクに対する能動的・意識的な計算処理を実行する。
        \item \textbf{SN（顕著性ネットワーク / Salience Network）}：\\
            島皮質前部（AI）および背側前帯状皮質（dACC）を中心とし、体内外の刺激から生存や目標にとっての「顕著性（salience）」を検知し、全脳の計算リソース配分を切り替える。
    \end{itemize}

    \chapter{Dynamics of Large-Scale Brain Networks: The Triple Network Model}

    Advances in resting-state functional magnetic resonance imaging (rs-fMRI) since the late twentieth century have driven a paradigm shift in neuroscience from "functional localization" to "large-scale functional connectivity networks" (Large-Scale Brain Networks). The brain operates not as an assembly of isolated functional areas, but as a dynamic system in which remotely distributed regions oscillate in synchrony.
    At its core lie the following three major networks:

    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{DMN (Default Mode Network)}:\
            A set of regions (including mPFC, PCC, and IPL) that synchronize during rest and introspective thought, and exhibit attenuated activity (task-negative correlation) during task performance. It governs self-referential thought, the retrieval of episodic memory, future simulation, and the construction of the self-narrative.
        \item \textbf{CEN (Central Executive Network)}:\
            Anchored in the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (dlPFC) and posterior parietal cortex (PPC), this network executes active, conscious computational processing directed toward external tasks, including working memory, logical inference, and problem solving.
        \item \textbf{SN (Salience Network)}:\
            Centered on the anterior insula (AI) and dorsal anterior cingulate cortex (dACC), it detects "salience" critical to survival and goals from internal and external stimuli, dynamically switching the allocation of computational resources across the whole brain.
    \end{itemize}

    \label{section:DMNの働き}

        \item \textbf{因果仮説の探索}：入力刺激および生じた情動出力に対し、納得し得る因果関係を過去の記憶から逆引きする。
        \item \textbf{未来の予測と更新}：不完全な情報環境下であっても、Ego中心のモデルに基づいて未来の不確実性を先回りしてシミュレーションし続ける。
    \end{enumerate}

    DMNは神経系の擬似ベイズ推論器として機能し、暫定的なナラティブ仮説が確定するまで環境および記憶から貪欲にサンプリングを行う。この際、計算資源の節約と不確実性の早期解消（エントロピー低減）を最優先するため、厳密な客観的事実よりも「直感的に辻褄が合う短絡的な仮説」が優先採択されやすい。

    \section{The Computational Essence of the DMN: Self-Defense and Narrative Generation}
    \label{section:DMN_functions}

    The essence of the DMN is an internal "self-defense module" dedicated to biological self-preservation and the maintenance of homeostasis. Its primary computational processes can be synthesized into the following four operations:

    \begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
        \item \textbf{Self-Referential Valuation of Stimuli}: Instantly categorizing inputs from the external world into "profit," "neutral," or "threat" relative to the "Ego."
        \item \textbf{Compilation of the Self-Narrative}: Generating contexts and narratives that cast the Ego as the central protagonist based on the nature of the stimuli. This encompasses the continuous surveillance of social hierarchies and one's relative standing within the group.
        \item \textbf{Heuristic Search for Causal Hypotheses}: Querying past episodic memory to retroactively construct a plausible causal link for the incoming stimuli and the resulting emotional outputs.
        \item \textbf{Future Prediction and Model Updating}: Even within an environment of incomplete information, relentlessly running proactive simulations of future uncertainties based on an Ego-centric model.
    \end{enumerate}

    The DMN operates as a pseudo-Bayesian inference engine within the nervous system, greedily sampling the environment and memory until a tentative narrative hypothesis solidifies. In doing so, it prioritizes the conservation of computational resources and the rapid resolution of uncertainty (entropy reduction) above all else; hence, it exhibits a systemic bias toward adopting "intuitively coherent, shortcut hypotheses" over rigorous objective facts.
    Predictions conclusively categorized as a "threat" through this process rapidly excite the limbic system—predominantly the amygdala—and trigger an emotional interruption of higher-order cognitive processes, forcibly initiating the fight-or-flight response.

    \section{The Triple Network Model and Dynamic Switching by the SN}

    Vinod Menon and Lucina Uddin established the `Triple Network Model'' through causal connectivity analyses, such as Granger causality and dynamic causal modeling (DCM). The CEN, which executes external cognitive tasks, and the DMN, which mediates introspection and rumination, maintain a mutually exclusive, anti-correlated relationship—a seesaw dynamic wherein the activation of one suppresses the other. The SN (particularly the right anterior insula, rAI) functions as the \textbf{`Causal Outflow Hub''} that governs the tilt of this seesaw.

    \input{src/figures/triple_network_seesaw.tex}

    When a subject is trapped in a self-referential loop of future anxiety or past regret (a DMN-dominant state), metabolic signals within the PCC and mPFC surge to their peaks. However, the instant the subject \textbf{sharply locks their attention onto direct interoceptive sensations}—such as tactile feedback from respiration or gravitational pressure on the soles of the feet—a cascade of neural dynamics unfolds:

    \begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
        \item \textbf{Burst Synchronization of the SN}: The anterior insula (AI) and dorsal anterior cingulate cortex (dACC) register the interoceptive signal, undergoing rapid and coordinated activation.
        \item \textbf{Downregulation of the DMN}: The activated SN issues robust inhibitory signals, forcibly suppressing the runaway activity of the DMN (PCC/mPFC) back to baseline levels.
        \item \textbf{Reallocation of Computational Resources}: Self-referential narrative synthesis is physically interrupted, and execution bandwidth is dynamically reassigned to the CEN (governing exteroception and working memory) or directly to the primary sensory cortices.
    \end{enumerate}

    In other words, the runaway activation of the DMN—manifesting as intrusive thoughts and anxiety—can be deterministically mitigated not through willpower, but via the physical trigger of direct somatic input routed through the SN.

\section{RAS: Reticular Activating System}
皮質においてこれら3大ネットワークが計算資源を奪い合う前段階として、そもそも感覚器から脳へと入る情報の総量を物理的に律速しているハードウェアが存在する。それがRASである。

騒がしい立食パーティーや雑踏の喧騒の中にいる場面を想像してみてほしい。
周囲にはグラスがぶつかる音、BGM、無数の人々の話し声が混ざり合い、物理的には強大な音の塊が鼓膜を激しく叩き続けている。
通常、それらの雑音は意識されることなく切り捨てられ、目の前の相手との会話にだけ焦点が合っている。

ところが、その喧騒のどこか遠くで、誰かが不意に「あなたの名前」を口にした瞬間、どうなるだろうか。
どんなに周囲の雑音が大きく、その声自体が小さかったとしても、意識は瞬時にそれを捉え、身体は反射的にその声の主の方へと振り向くはずだ。
心理学において「カクテルパーティー効果」として知られるこの現象は、私たちの脳が外界の音波を均一に記録するマイクではなく、特定の信号を選択的に増幅・遮断する物理フィルターを内蔵していることを証明している。

この生物学的ハードウェアの正体が、脳幹から大脳皮質へと投射される神経ネットワーク、すなわち\textbf{網様体賦活系（RAS: Reticular Activating System / 上行性網様体賦活系）}である。

RASは脳幹（中脳・橋・延髄）の網様体を起点とし、間脳（視床・視床下部）を経由して大脳皮質全体へと広く神経線維を伸ばしている。
その主要な機能は、意識の覚醒度をコントロールすることに加え、感覚器から絶え間なく流入する莫大な情報に対して「重要度に応じたゲートキーパー（門番）」として機能することにある。

この仕組みを情報の「帯域」という観点から捉えると、その役割が明瞭になる。
目や耳などの感覚器から入ってくる生の情報量は膨大であり、毎秒数億ビットにものぼると言われる。
一方で、私たちが「意識」として論理的に注意を向け、順を追って処理できる情報量は、毎秒わずか数十ビット程度にすぎない。
もし入ってくる刺激のすべてを均等に意識へ通してしまえば、脳の処理能力は瞬時にパンクしてしまう。

そこでRASは、意識の土台に立つファイアウォールとして常時稼働し、押し寄せる無数の刺激をふるいにかけ続ける。
その選別基準となるのが、過去の経験や生存本能に基づいてあらかじめ登録された「自分にとって重要な情報」である。
「自分の名前」「赤ん坊の泣き声」「危険を知らせる破裂音」といった重要項目は、脳の検疫リストに最優先シグナルとして刻まれている。

雑踏のざわめきの中にその特徴的な響きがほんのわずかでも紛れ込んだ瞬間、RASはそれを即座に検知し、大脳皮質へ「起きろ、これを見ろ」と覚醒シグナル（割り込み通知）を撃ち込む。
それによって意識のスポットライトが一気にその声へと向けられる。

名前を呼ばれて振り返るという反応は、私たちが意識して注意深く聞き耳を立てていたから起きたのではない。
意識の手前にあるRASというハードウェアが、意識を通さないバックグラウンドで外界の情報を監視し、一致する重要パターンを見つけ出した瞬間に注意を強制的に奪い取った結果なのである。

    \section{RAS: Reticular Activating System}

Before these three large-scale cortical networks compete for computational resources, a physical hardware gate already regulates the total influx of information from the sensory organs into the brain. That hardware is the \textbf{Reticular Activating System (RAS)}.

Imagine standing in the midst of a noisy buffet party or a crowded street.
Clinking glasses, background music, and countless overlapping voices blend into an immense acoustic onslaught that ceaselessly bombards the eardrums.
Normally, this cacophony is discarded without ever reaching conscious awareness, leaving your focus resting squarely on the conversation directly in front of you.

Yet what happens if someone far across the room suddenly utters your name?
No matter how deafening the ambient noise or how faint that single utterance may be, your consciousness latches onto it instantaneously, and your body reflexively turns toward the speaker.
Known in psychology as the ``cocktail party effect,'' this phenomenon demonstrates that our brain is not a passive microphone recording sound waves indiscriminately, but an active physical filter capable of selectively amplifying or attenuating specific signals.

The biological hardware driving this operation is the neural network projecting from the brainstem to the cerebral cortex: the \textbf{Reticular Activating System (RAS)}, or ascending reticular activating system.

Originating within the reticular formation of the brainstem (midbrain, pons, and medulla), the RAS projects diffuse neural pathways across the entire cerebral cortex via the diencephalon (thalamus and hypothalamus).
Beyond regulating levels of arousal and wakefulness, its primary computational function is to act as an importance-weighted gatekeeper against the relentless torrent of incoming sensory data.

Viewing this architecture through the lens of information bandwidth clarifies its systemic necessity.
The raw data flowing from sensory organs such as the eyes and ears is staggering, estimated at hundreds of millions of bits per second.
Conversely, the bandwidth of conscious, sequential cognitive processing tops out at a mere fraction of that—roughly several tens of bits per second.
If every sensory impulse were granted direct access to consciousness, the brain's computational capacity would suffer immediate catastrophic saturation.

The RAS therefore operates as a continuous, pre-conscious firewall, sifting through the deluge of incoming stimuli.
Its filtering criteria are dictated by pre-registered priorities established by past experience and evolutionary survival drives.
High-salience signals—one's own name, an infant's cry, or an abrupt acoustic transient signaling danger—are permanently etched into the brain's priority quarantine list.

The instant even a faint trace of such an acoustic signature surfaces from the background noise, the RAS detects it, instantly injecting an arousal signal—a hardware interrupt—into the cerebral cortex.
This interrupt forcibly redirects the beam of conscious attention toward the source.

Reflexively turning when your name is called is not the result of deliberate, conscious listening.
Rather, it is the deterministic outcome of upstream hardware: the RAS continuously monitors the external environment out-of-band and, the millisecond a registered pattern is matched, forcibly commandeers the spotlight of conscious attention.

    \section{The \fullAttentiveObserver: Integrating and Monitoring the Whole System}

The "Observer" is the computational function whereby the brain disengages from DMN-driven narrative synthesis, enabling the Salience Network (SN) and the frontoparietal network (CEN) to monitor interoceptive and exteroceptive signals as a pure, objective measuring apparatus.

We define the "Observer" as the neural function that maintains continuous meta-level monitoring over internal representations, the qualitative nature of input stimuli, the confidence ascribed to emerging narratives, their underlying causal attributions, and the entirety of internal dialogue. The Observer likewise takes as its observational target any physiological response or sensation arising within whatever bodily region attention is directed toward.
When this observing function fails to engage and "de-identification" from the Ego is not achieved, the organism remains trapped in the persistent illusion of conflating the Ego—or the narrative woven by the DMN—with "itself."
